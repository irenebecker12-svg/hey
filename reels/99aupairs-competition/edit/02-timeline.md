## Motion continuity across cuts

Match the direction of movement across each cut so the reel feels like one continuous rush:

- **0→2s** camera rising over water → **2s** cut into water. Down-into-up becomes up-into-down. This is the strongest cut in the reel; protect it.
- **4→5s** upward burst out of the water → **5s** into a rising orbit on the harbour.
- **11s** everything slams into 0.5s cuts. The rhythm change *is* the transition.
- **15s** montage energy releases into one long calm drone pull-back. Let it breathe.

## Type treatment

- **Face:** heavy condensed sans, all caps — Anton, Archivo Expanded Black, or the CapCut
  "Classic" bold. Whatever you use, it must survive a 6-inch screen at arm's length.
- **Size:** 86px for cards 1, 5, 6. 72px for cards 2, 3, 4, 7, 8. 34px for CTA. 22px terms.
- **Legibility:** white fill, 4px hard black outline, plus a soft 40%-opacity drop shadow.
  Not a semi-transparent box — that reads as a corporate ad, which is the one thing the
  brief rules out.
- **Entrance:** 4-frame scale pop, 108% → 100%. No fade-in; fades feel slow on a reel.
- **Exit:** 3-frame cut to nothing. Do not animate text out.

## Audio

- 120 BPM upbeat travel-pop, no vocals over the CTA.
- Duck the music 3dB from 15.6s so the end frame feels like a landing, not a fade.
- Optional accents: an underwater muffle-to-clear whoosh on the 4.00s surface cut, and one
  wind gust on the 15.00s drone pull-back. Nothing else — SFX clutter kills reel energy.

## Delivery

- H.264, 9:16, 1080×1920, 30fps, ~10–12 Mbps, AAC 128kbps 44.1kHz.
- Export **without** burned-in terms as a second version, in case you need a market variant.
- The 3 seconds from 15.00 is the only part you should ever change when you re-cut this
  for a different offer — shots 1–11 are an evergreen travel reel.
