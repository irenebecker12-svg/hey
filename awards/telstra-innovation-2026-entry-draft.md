## WRITTEN QUESTION 1 — Your business, the problem, and what makes it stand out
_(Covers: purpose and value proposition · target market and differentiation. Aim ~1,350 words; expand with a named family story where marked.)_

99aupairs is an Australian aupair placement agency. We connect families across the country with vetted, live in aupairs who provide flexible in home childcare that fits the hours families actually work. I founded the business in 2017, and today we have supported more than 1,000 Australian families with a team of five.

We exist to solve a specific, national problem. Centre based childcare does not cover before school, after school, school holidays, shift work, overnight work or irregular rosters. Families who live in those hours have two options. They stitch together several overlapping arrangements that never quite hold, or a parent, almost always the mother, reduces her hours or leaves work altogether. In home care has always been the natural answer, but it was slow to arrange, hard to verify and priced or serviced as a niche product for a small number of wealthy families. That is the gap we set out to close: access to trusted, flexible, in home care for ordinary working families.

I understand the problem because I lived it. My husband and I migrated to Australia with young children and no family nearby. We were building careers while raising a family without a support network, and welcoming an aupair into our home did not simply solve a logistics problem. It gave us back our time, our balance and our confidence as parents. I started 99aupairs so other families could feel that same shift.

What makes us stand out is not that we find an aupair. It is what we do around the match. Three things separate us from every other agency in Australia.

First, we stand behind the placement for a full year. Our continuity guarantee gives families unlimited rematches at no additional placement fee, with continuity of care between aupairs, so no family is ever left without support. Most agencies make a match and step back. We consider the match the beginning of our responsibility, not the end of it. That is why families stay with us for two to three years on average, and many for more than five.

Second, we are the best rated agency in the country, verifiably. We hold a 4.9 star rating across 190 Google reviews, from both the families we support and the aupairs we place. That rating held through the biggest operational rebuild in our history, which matters, because it proves the service did not slip while we transformed how we deliver it.

Third, and this is the part that is genuinely new, a five person company now delivers a premium, nationally available service that used to require a far larger agency. We did that by building our own technology, which is the subject of the next answer.

Our customers are working parents whose jobs do not fit a nine to five childcare window. Nurses on rotating shifts. Pilots. FIFO families. Small business owners. Parents of children with additional needs. One single mother raising a son with additional needs told me, "I haven't slept properly in seven years." She was not asking for childcare. She was asking for her life back. We matched her with an aupair who intuitively understood her son, and within weeks she told us she had it back. Families like hers are the reason the business exists.

Our value proposition is simple to say and hard to copy. We do not provide childcare. We give families the confidence to thrive. Every innovation we build has one purpose, helping families buy back time.

## WRITTEN QUESTION 2 — Your innovation and its measurable outcomes
_(Covers: use of technology · operating model and capability · new value creation · technology-enabled outcomes. This is our strongest answer. Aim ~1,400 words.)_

Our innovation is a suite of technology platforms that I designed and we built in house, despite my having no formal technical background. They are the reason five people can run a national, premium, guaranteed in home care service. Each one exists to remove a barrier that used to make trusted in home care slow, inconsistent or inaccessible. I will describe the four that matter, and the measurable outcome of each.

**A free public childcare subsidy calculator.** Australian families genuinely cannot work out what childcare actually costs them once the Child Care Subsidy is applied. Our calculator answers it in seconds, free, with no account and no sales gate. It compares the true out of pocket cost of childcare centres, family day care, nannies and aupairs, and we updated it the week the Government changed the subsidy rates in July 2026. Outcome: a piece of free public infrastructure used by thousands of families who will never become our customers. It was picked up by News Corp mastheads nationally. (Note for the entry: that coverage is of the calculator specifically, and we present it as such.)

**MAIA, asynchronous video interviewing.** Our candidates apply from the United Kingdom, Europe, Japan and South America, nine to eleven hours out of step with Australia. Previously we ran live interviews, endlessly rescheduled across time zones, and it took two to three weeks to reach a shortlist. Strong candidates accepted other offers before we could reach them. Now a candidate records a structured video interview whenever it suits them, and can re record any answer. Every interview is recorded and transcribed, so our assessment is auditable rather than remembered. Measurable outcome: for one candidate, Georgia from the UK, we moved from application to offer in approximately 24 hours, against a previous norm of two to three weeks. Families now meet matched, assessed candidates in days.

**DONNA, an after hours voice assistant.** DONNA answers calls after hours and on weekends, resolves common questions, and hands over to a team member whenever human attention is needed. Every call produces a transcript the team reviews. Measurable outcome: a parent researching childcare at 9pm gets a real answer at 9pm, from a five person business. Availability is no longer a function of headcount.

**ANNA, recruitment operations.** ANNA consolidates applications from our international sourcing channels, screens eligibility, organises documentation and prepares recruiter ready summaries. Measurable outcome: vetting is now consistent and complete on every single candidate, rather than varying with how busy the week was.

The point that ties these together, and the point I most want the judges to take away, is this. All four were built in house by a team of five, led by a founder with no technical background, and not one of them makes the final human decision about whether a person belongs in a particular family's home. That judgement stays with our recruiters. We automated the administration so our people have more time for the part that cannot be automated, which is care and human judgement.

The business outcome of the whole system is measurable and verified in Xero. In the 2026 financial year our total income grew 52.7 per cent, from $170,421 to $260,254, and our core placement revenue grew 528 per cent, from $31,818 to $199,986. We achieved that growth with no increase in headcount. The team was five people at the start of the year and five at the end. That is the definition of technology enabled scale: more families served, more revenue earned, the same number of people, because the technology carries the load that used to require hiring.

This is also a genuinely new business model for our sector. No aupair, nanny or childcare placement agency has previously built its own technology stack to deliver a guaranteed, nationally available service at this cost base. Agencies in our category typically either stay small and high touch because they cannot scale, or grow by adding staff and losing the personal relationship families come for. We found a third path. We scaled the relationship by building the technology that protects it. Every innovation we build has one purpose, helping families buy back time.

## WRITTEN QUESTION 3 — Financial performance, sustainability and growth
_(Covers: financial management and stability · growth and future direction · economic sustainability. Discloses the net loss openly, as required. Aim ~1,300 words.)_

I will give you the real numbers, because Telstra verifies the finances of successful entrants and because a founder who explains her own profit and loss confidently is worth more than one who avoids it.

In the 2026 financial year, total income grew 52.7 per cent, from $170,421 to $260,254. Our core placement revenue, which is the true measure of the business, grew 528 per cent, from $31,818 to $199,986. Gross profit grew 41.1 per cent, from $163,544 to $230,822. We delivered all of that with a team of five, unchanged across the year.

We also ran a net loss of $55,511 in FY26, against a loss of $52,863 in FY25. The loss was roughly flat while revenue grew by more than half, and it was deliberate. This year we chose to reinvest into building the platform and entering the market rather than to protect a profit line. The evidence is in where the money went. Marketing and branding rose from $387 to $35,026. Advertising rose 73 per cent, from $17,404 to $30,113. Our technology subscriptions rose 216 per cent, from $4,599 to $14,523, as we built and ran the platforms. And we brought the team onto payroll properly, with salaries and wages rising from zero to $46,762.

So the honest picture is a business growing revenue quickly, holding its loss flat while doing so, and reinvesting every available dollar into capability and market position. We report on revenue and capacity rather than profit for that reason, and we do it openly. A recurring guarantee model that keeps families for two to three years, and often more than five, gives us a revenue base that compounds. Each year's families do not churn out, they stay and they refer. Around 40 per cent of new families now arrive by referral.

Our growth plan builds on infrastructure we have already paid for. By 2030 we aim to support 10,000 Australian families. The same matching technology, the same vetting rigour and the same continuity guarantee extend naturally into two adjacent markets that are large and underserved. 99supports applies our model to families raising children with additional needs. Aceso Care applies it to helping older Australians remain safely in their own homes. Neither requires us to rebuild the engine. They reuse it, which is why growth compounds rather than restarts.

The financial story of 99aupairs is not a story of profit today. It is a story of a five person business that grew its core product more than fivefold in a year, kept its losses flat while doing it, and built durable technology assets that make the next stage of growth cheaper than the last. That is economic sustainability built deliberately, and I can evidence every figure in this answer from Xero.

## WRITTEN QUESTION 4 — Impact, community and future vision
_(Covers: social impact and contribution · community · collaborative ecosystems · 2030 vision. Aim ~1,300 words.)_

The impact of 99aupairs is measured in careers kept alive and families kept whole.

Every family we support is a parent who did not have to leave the workforce, and it is almost always the mother whose career is on the line. We measure ourselves by the careers we keep going. A pilot still flying. A nurse still in the role her community needs her in. A lawyer on the partnership track instead of stepping off it. These families did not need childcare in the ordinary sense. They needed care that flexed around shift work, early starts and unpredictable rosters, which is exactly what centre based care cannot offer. Keeping a skilled woman in her profession is an economic contribution to the country and a personal one to her family, and it is the contribution we make most often.

Our impact also reaches people who never pay us a cent. The free childcare subsidy calculator is public infrastructure. Any Australian family can use it to understand what care will cost them, whether or not they ever contact us. We built it and we maintain it, including rewriting it the week the Government changed the subsidy rates, because clarity about the cost of childcare is a public good.

We have a second community that most agencies overlook, which is the young aupairs themselves. Many arrive in Australia knowing nobody. We mentor them, we run welfare checks on them separately from the host family so they always have an independent point of contact, and we never abandon an aupair overseas if a placement ends. We also bring them together through community meetups. Two groups of women sit at the centre of this business. The mothers who want to stay in the careers they built, and the young women just beginning theirs.

On collaboration, we do not work alone. We operate an international sourcing network across the United Kingdom, Europe, Japan and South America that feeds candidates into our platforms. We maintain referral relationships with trusted partners in the parenting and family space. And our technology deliberately keeps a human partnership at its core: the platforms do the sourcing, screening and administration, and our recruiters, working with families, make the final judgement together. Our ambition over the next stage is to formalise these relationships into a stated partner ecosystem, because we know that is where our model can go further.

Our vision is concrete and time bound. By 2030 we aim to support 10,000 Australian families and to enable more than 20 million hours of trusted in home care. We will get there by extending the same technology and the same guarantee into families raising children with additional needs, through 99supports, and into older Australians who want to stay safely at home, through Aceso Care. The through line is unchanged from the day I started in 2017. We give families the confidence to thrive, and every innovation we build has one purpose, helping families buy back time.
