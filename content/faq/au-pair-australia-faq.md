## What is an au pair?
A live-in helper (usually 18–30) from overseas who provides in-home childcare and light
household help in exchange for a private room, meals and weekly pocket money, as part of a
cultural exchange. [Full guide →](/au-pair-australia)

## How much does an au pair cost in Australia?
The main cost is weekly pocket money plus board and meals, with an agency fee for screening,
matching and support. Unlike daycare, an au pair is a flat cost that doesn't rise per child.

## What visa do au pairs use?
Usually a Working Holiday visa (subclass 417) or Work and Holiday visa (subclass 462). Australia
has no dedicated au pair visa. Always check current conditions with the Department of Home Affairs.

## Do au pairs get the Child Care Subsidy (CCS)?
No. Au pairs are a cultural-exchange arrangement, not an approved childcare service, so CCS does
not apply. Compare after-subsidy daycare against all-in au pair cost. [Au Pair vs Daycare →](/compare/au-pair-vs-daycare)

## How is an au pair different from a nanny?
An au pair lives in and receives pocket money as cultural exchange; a nanny is usually a live-out
professional paid an award wage. [Au Pair vs Nanny →](/compare/au-pair-vs-nanny)

## Is it safe to find an au pair yourself?
It can be, but DIY puts all screening and risk on you. An agency verifies checks and supports the
placement. [Agency vs DIY →](/compare/au-pair-vs-agency-diy)
