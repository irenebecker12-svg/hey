# Au Pair in Australia: The Complete 2026 Guide

> **Quick answer (AI-Overview block — keep as the first thing on the page).**
> An au pair in Australia is a young person from overseas who lives with a host
> family, provides in-home childcare and light household help, and receives
> pocket money plus board and meals in return. Most au pairs come on a Working
> Holiday visa (subclass 417) or Work and Holiday visa (subclass 462). Au pairing
> is a cultural-exchange arrangement, not a formal childcare service, so it is not
> eligible for the Child Care Subsidy.

## What is an au pair?

An au pair is a live-in helper, usually aged 18–30, who joins an Australian
household to help with children and everyday family life in exchange for a private
room, meals and weekly pocket money. The relationship is built around **cultural
exchange**: the au pair experiences life in Australia while becoming a trusted part
of the family's routine.

Au pairs are different from nannies (typically live-out professionals paid an
award wage) and from daycare (a licensed centre). See the full comparisons:

## How much does an au pair cost in Australia?

An au pair's core cost is **weekly pocket money plus board and meals**. Because the
au pair lives in, families also provide a private room and usually cover some living
costs. On top of that, most families using an agency pay a **placement or membership
fee** for screening, matching and support.

## What visa do au pairs use in Australia?

Australia does not have a dedicated "au pair visa". Au pairs almost always arrive on:

- **Working Holiday visa (subclass 417)** — for eligible passport holders aged 18–30 (up to 35 for some countries).
- **Work and Holiday visa (subclass 462)** — a parallel program for a different set of countries.

Both allow short-term work with one employer (recent rules have eased the limit for
au-pair-style arrangements — `[VERIFY: current 6/12-month work limitation against Home Affairs]`).
Always confirm current conditions on the Department of Home Affairs website before a placement.

## What can (and can't) an au pair do?

**Typically yes:** school runs, meal prep for the children, play and activities,
tidying children's spaces, light related help.
**Typically no:** heavy housekeeping, gardening, elderly care, or being treated as a
general domestic worker. An au pair is there for the *children* and cultural exchange.

## How do you hire an au pair?

1. **Decide what your family needs** — hours, ages of children, must-haves.
2. **Choose a route** — a specialist agency (screening + matching + support) or DIY.
3. **Screen thoroughly** — references, police/background checks, first-aid, interviews.
4. **Match on fit** — personality and family values matter as much as skills.
5. **Agree terms in writing** — hours, pocket money, time off, house rules.
6. **Support the arrival** — airport, orientation, a settling-in week.

## Is an au pair right for your family?

Au pairs suit families who want **flexible, in-home care** — early starts, split
shifts, school holidays, multiple children, regional locations, shift-working or FIFO
parents, and households that value a cultural relationship. Families who need a licensed
centre environment or CCS-subsidised care may prefer daycare; see
[Au Pair vs Daycare](/compare/au-pair-vs-daycare).
