# Au Pair Agency vs Doing It Yourself

> **Quick answer.** A DIY au pair search (e.g. Facebook groups) is cheaper up front but
> puts all screening, matching, contracts and problem-solving on you. A specialist agency
> costs more but handles background checks, references, matching, paperwork and ongoing
> support — and steps in if a placement runs into trouble. For most families the safety and
> time saved outweigh the fee.

## At a glance

| | Agency | DIY / Facebook |
|---|---|---|
| Screening & checks | Done for you | Your responsibility |
| Matching | Guided, on family fit | Self-managed |
| Contracts & rules | Provided | You draft them |
| Support if it goes wrong | Yes — replacement/support | None |
| Up-front cost | Agency fee `[VERIFY]` | Lower/none |
| Risk | Lower | Higher |

## What an agency actually does for you

- Background and police checks, reference verification, first-aid confirmation.
- Structured video interviewing (99aupairs uses **MAIA**) and personality/fit matching.
- Family agreement templates covering hours, pocket money and time off.
- Ongoing support — and help re-matching if a placement doesn't work out.

## When DIY can work

DIY can suit experienced host families with time to screen carefully, who already know
what to check for and are comfortable managing contracts and any issues themselves.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it safe to find an au pair on Facebook?**
It can be, but you take on all screening and risk. An agency verifies checks and supports the placement.

**What do you get for an agency fee?**
Screening, matching, paperwork and ongoing support, including help if a placement needs to change.
